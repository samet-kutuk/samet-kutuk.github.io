The first Automated Mobility Summit, organized by [SAAM](https://automatedmobilitysummit.ch/), is behind us. I managed the AV showcasing activity, where two autonomous vehicles delivered a combined 110 km of rider experience.

[📷 View photo album on Google Photos](https://photos.app.goo.gl/U4uFdVUwyxGNd7z39)

I led the AV showcase at the Switzerland Innovation Park Zurich grounds: live autonomous rides with PIX Moving and LOXO on a closed track, clean runs, zero incidents. Watching people step out of a driverless vehicle with a different expression than when they got in is the whole point of doing this work. Some took their first AV ride; for others it was nothing new.

A few things stood out from the panel and my one-on-one conversations. The industry is done celebrating pilots — the ambition is scale, and some players are closer than people think, though meaningful pilots still have educational value and shouldn't be dismissed outright. European sovereignty in AV is a real tension, not just rhetoric: the technology gap is real today, and pragmatism is winning over ideology, which isn't necessarily bad, but Europe still wants sovereign, highly automated mobility solutions. Switzerland, and Zürich in particular, has the highest density of AI and engineering talent per capita in the world and is well placed to solve hard deep-tech problems, though the right autonomy solution will still need the right pricing and speed of building.

What struck me was that nobody at the event talked about collaboration and building on open source (or open core) for autonomy — even though open source is the foundational infrastructure of the modern internet and solved reliability and scalability problems there. I keep gently pushing people at events like this toward that reality, and I'm starting to see it land.

I also attended my first SAAM General Assembly meeting during the event, which was a good marker of being part of the ecosystem.
