The sixth episode of the Autoware Café covered autonomous racing across scales, from 1:10th racers to full-scale 250 km/h racecars.

{{< youtube Yr_mqi5cB00 >}}

I hosted three guests who sit at the intersection of research, racing, and robotics. Dr. Saeed Ahmed, Assistant Professor at the University of Groningen, supervised the team that won the latest RDW Self Driving Challenge in the Netherlands and runs Autoware on a Renault Twizy platform. Simon Sagmeister, Team Lead at TUM Autonomous Motorsports, leads the reigning champions of the Abu Dhabi Autonomous Racing League, who won a 20-lap final against six autonomous racecars at up to 250 km/h — their stack isn't built on Autoware, but the same TUM ecosystem powers EDGAR, which is. Po-Jen Wang, Software Developer at the Autoware Foundation, is a veteran autonomous racer, organizer of 1:10th scale competitions with The Roboracer Foundation (formerly F1Tenth), and a two-time winner of the Autonomous Karting Series Purdue National Grand Prix — his team built two separate Autoware flavors, one for 1:10th scale RoboRacers under strict hardware limits, and another for the go-kart that won at Purdue.

What ties the three together is that their breakthroughs come directly from pushing autonomous systems to the edge in research and racing settings. Racing exposes every weakness, every bottleneck, every systems-engineering problem — and if you can drive autonomously at the edge of physics, everyday autonomy gets a bit easier.
