I visited [ELROB 2026](https://elrob.org/elrob-2026/), a field robotics trial held in Thun, Switzerland, where autonomous machines worked through challenges like convoying, mule-style logistics, and search and rescue. I'd never heard of the event until a few months earlier — thanks to Peter and Roland for the tip.

{{< youtube xe81OSmndd0 >}}

The format earns respect immediately: quick, dense, informational presentations, no padding. After a short guided portion, the four-day event moved into actual fieldwork across five challenges and 21 teams:

Transport/Convoying tested multi-vehicle autonomous convoys through semi-urban and off-road conditions. Non-Urban Reconnaissance covered 3D mapping and detection of objects of interest. Transport/Mule had a robot learn a route by following a human guide, then shuttle autonomously between two camps — this was the most-entered challenge. Search & Rescue/CasEvac required locating and retrieving a casualty in rough, partially blocked terrain. Reconnaissance of ERW involved mapping, inspecting, and flagging explosive remnants of war across mixed indoor/outdoor environments.

I was there Monday and Tuesday; I missed Wednesday and relied on colleagues who stayed to fill me in. The trial took place in a military complex — a proper facility that made these scenarios possible at full scale. Quadruped, wheeled, tracked, and palletted robots of every size were there, tethered or otherwise, rehearsing before their slot. The environment felt real because the context was real.

There was food and drinks in the evenings, teams unwinding after the day. I did my military service in a complex not unlike this one, and I could never have imagined myself at a grill with a beer inside that kind of perimeter — it felt good-weird, a version of that place I didn't know was possible.

Some teams have been coming to ELROB for years, and the experience gap between veterans and newcomers was almost visible. But any serious work in this domain gets appreciated here, regardless of where a team is starting from.

Thomas told me Frank E. Schneider had been instrumental in organizing this and many former editions — hats off to him.

I wrote a longer piece on this, including Wednesday's action through the eyes of colleagues who stayed, on [Substack](https://thecommunitypractitioner.substack.com/p/elrob-2026-notes-from-the-largest).
