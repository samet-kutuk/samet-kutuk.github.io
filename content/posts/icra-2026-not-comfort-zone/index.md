I went to [ICRA 2026](https://2026.ieee-icra.org/) with Nomadic AI, and also co-organized a ROS meetup during the conference. Two things about it were genuinely uncomfortable, and both taught me something.

[📷 View photo album on Google Photos](https://photos.app.goo.gl/U9rT2pk5UCHsWz3U6)

{{< youtube _Os7r2rC2bM >}}

The first was an organizational shift. I'm coming from years in the autonomous vehicles space, where I knew the rhythm, the players, and how things work. Moving into a role at NomadicML, where I often need to acquire new knowledge fast and work in an asynchronous, AI-tool-heavy way, has been an adjustment. I like to think things through carefully, and I'm still recalibrating that instinct to match the pace of a young, ambitious team.

The second was a domain gap. I assumed robotics would be a short hop from AVs. It isn't. Learned systems in robotics aren't optional — they're fundamental in ways AVs haven't fully embraced. A humanoid with 40+ degrees of freedom can't be hardcoded. That sent me back to basics, working through open-source robotics courses from Stanford, MIT, and ETH Zürich to close the gap.

At the expo, humanoids were everywhere, though most are headed to research labs rather than factories or homes — the research community is candid about that, even if some LinkedIn narratives aren't. What gave me some relief was that nobody has this fully figured out. The debates and unsolved problems are real, which reminded me of the early days of AVs — everyone senses the inflection point, nobody knows which path gets there first.

Open source had a strong pulse at the event: KE:SAI and the FTM Institute of Automotive Technology at TUM on workshops and talks, Franka Robotics dropping LABS, and AIRoA building an open non-profit data initiative. Those are the signals worth following.

The ROS meetup drew 300 registrations. Thanks to Katherine for the work organizing it, and to Intrinsic, Foxglove, and prefix.dev for co-hosting.
