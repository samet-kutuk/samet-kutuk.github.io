I went to the inaugural [Davos Tech Summit](https://davostechsummit.com/) as a guest of Kanton Zürich. My biggest takeaway wasn't about any single robot or demo — it was that integration is the hard problem the industry still has to solve, and that whoever works on that layer in an open, democratizing way will matter more than whoever builds the flashiest standalone system.

{{< youtube kmnYdqn9v08 >}}

Robot intelligence and skills aren't the end game. A capable robot that doesn't fit into its surrounding ecosystem is a deployment headache for both suppliers and users. Robotics carries decades of expectations with it, and those expectations still need to be met. Ryan Gariepy made that point well to the room.

There's real potential in robots, including humanoids, but the deployment targets should be deliberate: industrial settings, dangerous environments, search and rescue, hazardous inspection, rehabilitation, and assistance. Yasuhisa Hirata from Tohoku University made a strong case for the latter. Productivity applications — cleaning, restocking, repetitive logistics — are also genuinely valuable without needing to be oversold.

I rode both the Autoware-powered PIX Moving RoboBus and Bertrandt Group's Bumblebee as they cruised through Davos. Both were smooth and confidence-inspiring, until road construction appeared on the route and each had to switch to manual control. That was an honest moment — it showed exactly where the most important engineering work still needs to happen.

RobotCity was a highlight: fifty robots meeting their future collaborators, with demos that were impressive without pretending to be more than they were.

Europe has the industrial know-how, the governance track record, and the talent. The limitations are real, but so is the foundation. If played right, the competitiveness is there — but the associations representing this work need to be assertive, not just collegial.

Congrats to Sacha Ghiglione and Tilman Eberle on a strong inaugural edition. The drone show over Davos was a proper Swiss send-off.
