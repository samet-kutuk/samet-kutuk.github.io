I was in Brussels for the inaugural [PAVE Europe Conference](https://pavecampaign.org/pave-europe-conference-2025-report/), representing the Autoware Foundation. It's one of several new AV gatherings this year, alongside our own AutowareCon. The event brought together industry, public-sector, and mobility leaders to discuss how autonomous mobility fits into Europe's regulatory, inclusive, and long-term deployment landscape, with organizations like Waymo, Zeekr, Ruter, MOIA, Pony.ai, EIT Urban Mobility, SAAM, Transdev, ERTICO, FISITA, EasyMile, and several European cities and ministries in the room.

A few things stood out. EU pilots continue, but public transport operators and authorities still look to the US and China for real-scale deployment lessons — the "learning by doing" mindset is growing. The European Commission supports large-scale AV testbeds, but long-term funding remains unclear, and testbeds need to lead to actual deployment rather than staying isolated. Regulation is still the sticking point, with a strong call for EU-wide harmonization — one participant put it bluntly: "27 rulebooks won't help anyone." Robotaxi still dominates the conversation, but people-movers are needed to fill public transport gaps, and logistics and yard automation are accelerating, with global operators asking when similar EU deployments will be possible.

Two sessions focused on accessibility for disabled and blind passengers — boarding, interfaces, remote assistance, inclusive UX — with the message that accessibility has to be built in from day one, not retrofitted. Switzerland's new ordinance, in effect since March 1, 2025, drew attention, though whether it becomes a model or reveals gaps remains to be seen. Speakers stressed that simulation alone isn't enough — real routes, riders, and constraints matter, and one useful benchmark floated for what "scale" means was around 100,000 robotaxi vehicles.

What surprised me most was that open source wasn't mentioned once, despite its role in the software-defined vehicle world. I expect open-source AV to show up in Europe sooner than people currently expect.

Congrats to Guido Di Pasquale and team for organizing a well-structured event.
