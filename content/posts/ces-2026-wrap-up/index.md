CES is intense by design — a lot happens in a short window, and what matters most is what you learn by being there, talking to people, and comparing notes across the ecosystem.

We hosted our third consecutive CES Reception at the TIER IV booth, with 149 participants: a mix of OEMs, Tier-1s, SoC and platform providers, ADAS/AV software companies, and Autoware Foundation members. The event was sponsored by AMD, Arm, NomadicML, and TIER IV as gold sponsors, and Deepen AI, eSync Alliance, and Red Hat as silver sponsors.

We showed the latest Open AD Kit demo at the TIER IV booth and at the Connected Vehicle Systems Alliance (COVESA) event at the Bellagio, where I represented SOAFEE with our Open AD Kit blueprint showcase. This iteration focused on mixed-critical orchestration and a Minimum Risk Maneuver concept. Alongside the demo, we released the fifth-generation Open AD Kit handout and, for the first time, a whitepaper documenting the scope, architecture, and collaborative development behind the work.

I carried two large suitcases of swag from Istanbul to hand over to Autoware Foundation members — a difficult trip logistically, but handing over member certificates and swag bags in person was worth it. We met 30+ members across the show floor this way.

I also put together a short video interview series with our reception sponsors, focused on how each of them is using Autoware today.

More broadly, AI, physical AI, autonomy, and robotics were everywhere on the show floor, but the tone felt different this year — fewer headline-grabbing demos, more discussion around shipping, integration, and operational reality. OEM booth presence was limited, but OEM teams were very present, and much of the visible momentum came from SoC providers and ADAS/AV software companies. Open software kept moving closer to the center — NVIDIA's Alpamayo reasoning models and continued momentum around Eclipse SDV both reflect a shift from open as an experiment to open as a platform decision.
