I contributed to the Autoware End-to-End roadmap document before its release, signing off on it ahead of publication. The Autoware Foundation put out a [56-page strategic report](https://autoware.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Autoware-E2E-Report_Release.pdf) laying the groundwork for open-source, end-to-end autonomous driving.

Traditional robotics stacks are hitting their limits, neural networks and AI-first, data-centric design are unlocking new capability, and safety and certifiability have to be built in from day one to matter for real-world deployment. The report introduces a new Level 4+ nomenclature that goes beyond today's standards, a stepwise evolution from modular planning to fully learned driving without breaking changes, the Generator-Selector framework for explainability and safe trajectory selection, and a multi-year roadmap spanning personal cars, cargo pods, trucks, shuttles, buses, and off-road vehicles. It also covers methods like sim-to-real neural style transfer and photorealistic world simulation, and applies standards like ISO 26262, ISO 21448, and ISO 34503 to build a credible path to safe deployment.

It's meant as a blueprint for the next decade of open-source mobility — a starting point for OEMs, Tier 1s, startups, regulators, and researchers who want to shape where autonomy goes next.
