The fourth episode of the Autoware Café looked at scaling autonomy across platforms with the AutoDRIVE Ecosystem, developed at Clemson University.

{{< youtube BYaQ8u0tJ94 >}}

I hosted Chinmay and Tanmay Samak, twin brothers and PhD candidates at Clemson who created AutoDRIVE — a unified framework for modeling and simulating digital twins of autonomous platforms across scales and operational design domains, with real2sim calibration for streamlined development and validation. We talked about how Autoware can be deployed within the AutoDRIVE digital twin framework, ODD-specific objectives for autonomy stacks across vehicle types, the role of digital twins in virtual, hybrid, and physical testing, and how sim2real transfer can accelerate autonomy R&D globally. We also touched on the rugged research vehicle platforms Clemson is building and how AutoDRIVE powers The Roboracer Foundation's Sim Racing League.

Deploying and validating autonomy algorithms across scales isn't simple — scale-specific dynamics, sensor integration complexities, computational constraints, regulatory and safety considerations, environmental variability, and interactions with other traffic participants all get in the way. That's exactly the gap AutoDRIVE is built to close.
